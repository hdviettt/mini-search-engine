What moved, what did not, and what I could not fix

Every change measured with eval/run.py against the frozen baseline. Two were reverted because the numbers said so.

Search is about fifteen times faster and answers a materially better set of queries. nDCG@10 went 0.8698 to 0.8956 and MRR 0.7963 to 0.8285 over the session, misspelled queries went from the worst intent to nearly the best, and zero-result precision reached a clean 1.0. The single largest win was not a ranking change at all: the reranker was spending 6.4 seconds per search because ONNX was sizing its thread pool to the host's cores rather than the container's quota.

What worked

ONNX was running a 48-thread pool on an 8-CPU quota13x

Search had gone to 8.4 seconds. The explain trace put 6360 ms of that in reranking and 1813 ms in snippets, with every other stage under 70 ms. os.cpu_count() reports 48 on this host; the container's cgroup quota is 8. ONNX Runtime sizes its intra-op pool from the visible count, so it was building 48 threads to share 8 CPUs.

batch of 40 pairs, sequence length 128, median of 5 48 threads (the default) 3322 ms 83.0 ms per candidate 16 threads 424 ms 10.6 ms per candidate 8 threads (the quota) 247 ms 6.2 ms per candidate 4 threads 556 ms 13.9 ms per candidate 1 thread 1673 ms 41.8 ms per candidate

That 16 is worse than 8 is the tell: past the quota the threads contend rather than work. This also closes something CLAUDE.md had left open, where the gap between 10 ms per candidate locally and the deployed cost was recorded as "not been isolated". It was this.

Snippets were the other 1.8 seconds, for a duller reason: generate_snippet stems every word of every candidate sentence, roughly 10,000 Porter calls per search, and ran its cleaning regexes over the whole document rather than the 3000-character window it reads. Memoising stem and bounding the cleaning took it to 24 ms.

total 8382 ms -> 284 ms reranking 6360 -> 190 snippets 1813 -> 24
Spell correction had never actually run+0.365

It was wired to /api/search/explain only. Where it did run, the trigger was total_results == 0, which cannot detect a partial misspelling: in "premeir league" the word "league" matches thousands of documents on its own, so the count is never zero. Vocabulary membership is the right test.

Fixing the trigger got two of three test queries working and left premeir league broken, because of a second bug. In a 200k-word vocabulary a great many words sit exactly two edits from any typo, and if d < best_dist kept whichever one set iteration reached first. Candidates are now ranked by distance then by how often the word appears in page titles. misspelled went 0.6064 to 0.9715.

Nonsense queries now return nothing0.60 → 1.00

BM25 admitted any document matching any single term, so sourdough starter hydration ratio matched football pages on "starter" alone, a starter being also a player who starts. A document now needs ceil(0.34 × distinct terms) of them, floored at two, for queries of three terms or more.

A ratio of 0.5 was measured first and cost multi_term 0.049 by demanding three hits on ordinary six-word questions. The floor of two is what does the work.

Crawl scheduling was acting as a relevance signal+0.016

The freshness multiplier takes COALESCE(last_checked_at, crawled_at), which is when we last fetched a page and not when it was written. With a 0.5 floor and 1.2 ceiling that was a 2.4x swing, so a single crawl that added 300 news pages demoted five thousand reference pages by construction. offside rule started returning comment pieces above Offside (association football) because they had been fetched more recently.

Narrowed to 0.85 to 1.05. Worth +0.0158 nDCG@10, with informational and multi_term each gaining about 0.05. The band widens again when the input is a published date, which is the real fix and is not done.

Sources chosen on extractable text instead of bytescorpus

The news tier was configured correctly and produced nothing, and the frontier was only half the reason. BBC, ESPN, the Guardian, Goal and premierleague.com render their article text in the browser: they answer 200 with hundreds of kilobytes and plenty of links, and the crawler stores a shell. espn.com/soccer/ yields 49 characters of body.

pages that passed the quality gate, over pages crawled mirror.co.uk 67/68 = 99% median article 603 words independent.co.uk 62/69 = 90% median article 345 words skysports.com 72/145 = 50% --- www.uefa.com 11/73 = 15% www.theguardian.com 1/60 = 2% www.bbc.com 0/24 = 0% www.espn.com 0/46 = 0% www.goal.com 0/39 = 0%

I put those five on the list myself, earlier the same day, by measuring response bytes and link counts. That is the wrong test and the config now says so. Replacing them on measured article yield brought in football365 (607 words), teamtalk (428) and caughtoffside (407). After one crawl: caughtoffside 19 of 19 indexed, mirror 85 of 86, independent 81 of 88.

Note the trap in the measurement itself: football365's hub page yields 44 words. Hub pages are link lists everywhere and must not be what gets measured.

One empty response was ending the whole crawlcrawl

html.fromstring("") raises ParserError, and bbc.com/sport/football/european answers 200 with an empty body. The scheduler caught that as a failed schedule and abandoned the run, so scheduled crawls had been dying early on whatever empty document they met first. The corpus was not growing for a reason that never looked like a crash.

What I tried and reverted

Both of these were reasonable, and the eval disagreed. Recording them so they are not retried blind.

Relaxing RERANK_MIN_SCORE from −8.0 to −10.5Reverted

The threshold deletes 60% of everything the cross-encoder scores, which was tolerated only because it was also the sole defence against nonsense queries. Minimum-should-match took that job over, so relaxing it looked free.

nDCG@10 0.9096 -> 0.8948 navigational 0.6499 -> 0.5780 latency p95 1.2s -> 4.8s

The filter is doing real work beyond rejecting nonsense: it keeps weak candidates out of the head and out of snippet generation. Left at −8.0, now env-overridable so it can be swept without a redeploy.

An authority prior on the reranked headReverted

Aimed at the news-over-encyclopedia problem below. PageRank knows which pages are authoritative but only reaches the combined score, which selects candidates rather than ordering the head, so it was reapplied in logit space.

nDCG@10 0.8956 -> 0.8816 multi_term 0.8827 -> 0.8038

And it did not move either query it was written for. Normalised PageRank inside a 500-page candidate pool evidently does not separate a reference article from its neighbours the way it separates Wikipedia from news across the whole corpus. Worth retrying against raw rather than pool-normalised PageRank, but not on this evidence.

Still open

navigational at 0.5780 is mostly not a ranking problem. Two of its five queries are espn soccer and fbref premier league stats, whose labels are espn.com and fbref.com. ESPN renders its content in JavaScript and yields zero indexable pages; fbref answers 403. Those two queries are unanswerable by construction, which caps the whole intent near 0.6 whatever the ranker does. The eval is measuring something the corpus cannot contain, and that is worth fixing in eval/queries.yaml rather than in the ranker.

News still outranks the encyclopedia on definitional queries. offside rule returns an Independent column about VAR first and does not have Offside (association football) in the top six; var video assistant referee leads with a TeamTalk story. News prose mentions the words more often than an encyclopedia entry defines them, and the cross-encoder scores topical relevance only. The authority prior was the obvious fix and it failed. This is the most valuable thing left.

Freshness should come from a published date. It is currently a crawl date, which is why it had to be turned down rather than tuned. Extracting article:published_time or JSON-LD datePublished at parse time would make the signal mean what it claims and let the band widen again.

Embedding coverage. Chunk embeddings were at 62% when the session started, which is the vector half of AI Overview retrieval running on well under the full corpus. A backfill is in progress. It is cheap, roughly 10M tokens on voyage-3-lite, but it does spend your API credit.
